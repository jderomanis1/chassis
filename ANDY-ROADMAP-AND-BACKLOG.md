**Handoff on November 19, 2026.** Andy, the question you started with was whether Homer could help run several ventures from one shared set of records. The prototype proves a person and a local AI client can work on the same fictional task and see the change on a zoomable board. The next proof is yours to choose. ## Try this first

1. Open the seven-venture demo on your computer. The transition kit includes a 44-second walkthrough and a launcher. Run `sh run-local-demo.sh` from the extracted kit, then open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/`. For a GitHub-only checkout, use the separate setup in [Start here](START-HERE-ANDY.md#where-to-begin-on-your-own-machine).
2. Ask Homer, “Across these ventures, what needs my attention, and what would you do first?” The seed gives him two facts he can check independently. Ridgeview’s second launch review is blocked pending supplier approval. Atlas’s third launch review depends on Ridgeview’s third operations review. Ask for the record IDs and his reasoning. [Fictional records generator](src/chassis/demo.py)
3. Pick one recommendation yourself. Once you approve that fictional edit, have Homer change only the chosen task’s next step from your own compatible desktop client. Keep its card open. Use the original five-second target and time the open card from save to visible update. Check that the version advances once, Git shows one chief-of-staff commit on that file, and you can find the same change tomorrow. A prepared local MCP client has already demonstrated the underlying write. Your own Homer client is the part still to prove. [Local agent-to-canvas test](tests/e2e/live.spec.mjs)

## Roadmap Andy can reorder

These are the milestones from the November 17 roadmap, expressed as choices for an independent build. The week numbers identify the earlier proposal. Andy chooses what to keep, who owns it and when to start. This handoff assigns no further delivery work to Joe.

| Original milestone | What remains for Andy to choose | Evidence that would make it worth keeping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 0. Working mock | Try one desktop decision on fictional data. Local storage, connector and canvas already work. | You choose the task, watch Homer update it, and find it again the next day. |
| Week 1. Independent setup | Use your own local checkout or restore the bundled history into your own private repository. Choose who holds credentials and backups. | You can launch the demo and recover its history without access to Joe’s accounts. |
| Week 2. Voice, phone and security | Choose desktop-only or add view commands by voice and a phone inbox. The proposed phone design uses a filtered read-only copy, with add-only requests queued while the laptop is off. | Desktop voice changes only the view. On the phone path, a queued fictional card arrives exactly once and restricted content stays out of the copy. Review access, storage and backups before real data. |
| Weeks 3–4. Venture pilot and links | After the security review and your authorization, start with a checklist for two or three ventures. Expand toward seven only if useful. Decide how ordinary links differ from dependencies, whether tasks need to move between parents, and whether an end-of-day file drop suffices for mobile intake. | The pilot matches your checklist with zero missing or invented items. Moving a task is reversible, a dependency loop is refused, and importing the same file twice creates no duplicate. |
| Week 5. Model and agent choice | Compare two clients on the same updates, try a second separately scoped identity, or add a manual override for venture health if the computed color misses how you run it. | Two clients produce the same record changes. The second identity can see only its granted ventures. |
| Weeks 6–7. Recovery, history and Miro | Restore an encrypted backup with a key you hold. If old conversations matter, start with a sample before importing the roughly 45 threads and siloed projects. Choose one-way Miro export only if you use it. | Restore reproduces the vault. Each imported item links to a venture or an “unsorted” list and a repeat import creates no duplicate. A scratch Miro export updates one card in place and a second unchanged export performs zero writes. |
| Week 8. Independent acceptance | Choose the workflows you rely on and run them yourself on a clean install with your keys and runbook. | You can repeat them without Joe, restore the records and decide which remaining defects matter. |

**Smallest viable scope.** If desktop is where you make decisions, keep the store, connector and canvas. First prove the fictional workflow above, then decide whether a reviewed two-venture pilot earns its place. If capturing ideas away from the desk is the deciding factor, move the actual-phone trial ahead of that pilot. An in-canvas chat, phone editing or deletes, two-way Miro sync, specialist agents and fully custom schemas or themes are separate expansion choices.
